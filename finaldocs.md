# Final Project - Dancing Fish

## Explanation

For my final project, I decided to try a new algorithm and start with the boids algorithm. I knew that I wanted to do something with interaction and decided that having it be music would be fun. In past projects, I had seen a pre-loaded audio file that controlled things. I decided that I wanted to allow users to upload any audio file that they wanted to and have the boids react to it. I was able to get audio uploaded and playing, but I wasn’t sure how to pull aspects of the music out of it. I was pointed in the direction of the Meyda javascript library and that ended up being super helpful. 

When looking into Meyda, there were a ton of audio features that I could pull from. After messing around with a few, I decided to stick to the perceptual features. These are features that Meyda flags as matching how humans perceive things rather than the exact scientific wave math. I decided to choose loudness which is the overall frequency perception as outlined by the Bark Scale. I also chose to use perceptual sharpness which is also based on the Bark Scale. The example given in the documentation is identifying the sharpness difference between a snare-drum and a bass-drum. 

Once I had isolated these values using Meyda, I used them to alter certain aspects of the algorithm. I used sharpness to control the sizes of the boids. When there is a sharp sound, the boids grow in size. When sharpness is low, they are smaller. The first place I used loudness is to create a bit more randomness in the boids movement pattern. When loudness hits a certain threshold, I add a velocity kick to each of the boids. The next place I used loudness is to control the transparency of the boids. As the audio gets louder, the boids get more opaque. When things are quiet, they are pretty transparent. Lastly, I used loudness to control the amount of feedback that the boids have. As the song is louder, the longer their trails are. When there is no song playing or when the song is quiet, there is no feedback trail. 

After getting all of that working, I added a tweakpane panel where users can pause, play, and restart the song. I also added the ability to upload a new audio file to try. As I was messing around with this. I realized that my alternations had made it so that when there is no music playing, the boids scatter and sit by the edges of the screen. This gave me the idea of theming the simulation. I decided to go with dancing fish. I made the background a dark blue and had all of the boids be various shades of orange. When there is no music playing, the fish shy away. When there is music, they come out and dance.
